What am I looking at?

This screen watches city traffic live. Vehicles (mostly buses) send their GPS position every few seconds. The map is divided into small hexagon-shaped patches, and each patch is coloured by how much of its traffic is crawling. When something looks wrong, the system can ask an AI assistant to take a closer look and draw a circle (a “monitored area”) around the trouble spot. Every suggestion from the AI is checked by a safety checker before it is used.

Update — one GPS location report sent by a vehicle.
Patch — one small hexagon on the map.
Slow traffic — vehicles moving slower than 10 km/h.
Fast processing — the quick route that handles every update straight away, with no AI involved.
AI assistant — the slower, thinking part that investigates suspicious patches.
Monitored area — a circle on the map that the system keeps a special eye on.
Worker — one copy of a program that handles a share of the incoming data.
Backlog — updates still waiting to be processed. Small means the system is keeping up.
ms (millisecond) — a thousandth of a second. A lower number means a faster system.

Kafka topics & partitions

Incoming data is sorted into channels (for example “raw location updates”). Each channel is split into numbered lanes so that several workers can read it at the same time. The number next to each lane is how many updates have passed through it so far.
